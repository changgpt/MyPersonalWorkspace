# On the name

## "Filofacts" is closer to the trademark, not further from it

This is the thing worth knowing before anything else: **"Filofax" is itself a
contraction of "file of facts."** The company sold the product as *File of
Facts* and registered the shortened form as a trademark in 1930 (UK; a
separate US registration followed in 1984).

So "Filofacts" doesn't sidestep the mark — it reassembles the exact phrase the
mark was coined from, for a product in the same category (organisers, diaries,
planners). Infringement turns on *likelihood of confusion*, which is roughly
"similar mark + related goods". Filofacts scores high on both halves.

Also relevant to the logo: the registered family includes an **"F" device
mark** ("F FILOFAX SYSTEM ORGANISER"). A bookmark with an F in it, for a
notebook app, is therefore the riskiest icon on the sheet — not because the
shape is owned, but because the letter is.

**I'm not a lawyer and this isn't legal advice.** What is plainly true:

- **Running it on 127.0.0.1 for yourself: no practical risk.** Trademark law
  governs use *in commerce*. A local tool you never distribute isn't that.
- **Risk begins the moment it's public** — a domain, an App Store listing, a
  GitHub repo people install, a product someone pays for.
- If it ever goes that way, get a real clearance search. Don't rely on this
  file.

## Names that keep the wit and are clear of it

All three are bookbinding or reference-book words, so they still pair with a
spine mark. None has been trademark-cleared — same caveat as above.

- **Quire** — the bookbinding unit: a gathering of folded sheets sewn into the
  spine. A notebook *is* quires on a spine, so it sits perfectly with the
  spine logo. Short, odd enough to be memorable.
- **Vade** — from *vade mecum*, "go with me": the term for a handbook you
  carry about. Describes the product exactly.
- **Factotum** — someone who does every kind of work. Keeps the "fact"
  sound you liked, means something close to what the app does.

Also considered: **Verso** (the left-hand page), **Colophon** (the printer's
note at the end of a book), **Foolscap** (a paper size).
